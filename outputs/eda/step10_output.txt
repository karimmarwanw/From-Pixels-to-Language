
========================================================================
STEP 10 - Augmentation  |  normalization: imagenet (mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std [0.229, 0.224, 0.225])
========================================================================
TRAIN transform (random, new every time an image is loaded):
  ToRGB()
  RandomResizedCrop(size=(224, 224), scale=(0.5, 1.0), ratio=(0.75, 1.3333), interpolation=bilinear, antialias=True)
  RandomHorizontalFlip(p=0.5)
  RandomRotation(degrees=[-15.0, 15.0], interpolation=nearest, expand=False, fill=(124, 116, 104))
  ColorJitter(brightness=(0.8, 1.2), contrast=(0.8, 1.2), saturation=None, hue=None)
  ToTensor()
  Normalize(mean=[0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std=[0.229, 0.224, 0.225])

VAL / TEST transform (fixed, identical every time):
  ToRGB()
  Resize(size=256, interpolation=bilinear, max_size=None, antialias=True)
  CenterCrop(size=(224, 224))
  ToTensor()
  Normalize(mean=[0.485, 0.456, 0.406], std=[0.229, 0.224, 0.225])

========================================================================
Why each augmentation is used or rejected
========================================================================
USED:
  + RandomResizedCrop               scale [0.5, 1.0]  bird size/position varies; images ~500x375 -> 224 (steps 3, 5); min scale 0.5 so the bird is rarely cropped out
  + RandomHorizontalFlip            p=0.5             birds face left or right; a mirrored bird is the same species
  + RandomRotation                  +-15 deg          tilted camera / perched at an angle; corners filled with the mean color
  + ColorJitter brightness/contrast 0.2/0.2           lighting varies a lot: 14 very dark, 29 very bright images (step 3)
REJECTED:
  - RandomVerticalFlip              birds are never upside down; would create unrealistic images
  - Hue / saturation jitter         color separates look-alike species (gull bills, warbler throats; steps 5-6); 97.6% of captions mention a color
  - RandomGrayscale                 removes color, the key cue; only 5 of 5994 train images are gray
  - Strong crops (scale < 0.5)      a small bird in a big frame (very bright images, step 3) could be cut out
  - Large rotations                 birds are photographed roughly upright; large angles are unrealistic
